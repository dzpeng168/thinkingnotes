# Insight into Human Nature: Peeling the Onion

> **The Onion Model · 8 layers of insight.** Which layer is your understanding of people at?
>
> From the outermost "behavior" to the innermost "self", peel layer by layer: behavior → coping style → emotion → feelings about feelings → beliefs → expectations → longings → self.

**Subject**: Zhou, a cross-functional designer who blew up in our last two design reviews.

---

## Layer 1 · Behavior / Event

> Objectively describe what happened

- At Thursday's review, I raised 3 changes; he cut me off on the spot: "You people always drop requirements at the last minute."
- He skipped the post-review retro, went straight back to his desk, and put on headphones.
- Observed fact: the requirement doc was sent Wednesday 21:40 — less than 14 hours before the review.

## Layer 2 · Coping Style

> Identify the other person's coping patterns / stances

- Classic **blaming stance**: leads by pointing out the other's fault ("you people always…"), positioning himself as the wronged party.
- His pattern under pressure: withdraws after conflict (leaving, headphones) — he needs to digest alone.

## Layer 3 · Emotion / Feeling

> Catch the precise emotion

- The surface layer is **anger**, but more precisely: **the hurt of feeling dismissed** and **anxiety about losing control** — he wasn't angry at me; he was angry that his professional time wasn't respected.

## Layer 4 · Feelings about Feelings

> Empathize with the deeper feelings

- He feels **remorse** about "blowing up publicly" — losing his temper doesn't match his self-image as a "professional designer," and that remorse is something he can't voice.

## Layer 5 · Thoughts / Beliefs

> Point directly at the internal views

- Core belief: "Last-minute requirements = product doesn't respect design = I'm being treated as a tool."
- Perspective filter: "Requirements given in advance" is the baseline of professionalism — his key criterion for judging whether a partner is reliable.

## Layer 6 · Expectations

> Understand the other's expectations

- Of himself: stable, high-quality output, never thrown off rhythm by last-minute changes.
- Of me: requirements shared 2 days early, major changes discussed privately first.
- Of the work: reviews should "confirm", not "surprise".

## Layer 7 · Longings

> Resonate with the deep needs

- The underlying longing: **to be respected and recognized** — his professional time and judgment deserve to be taken seriously.

## Layer 8 · Self

> See through to the other's self

- How he sees himself: someone who stands on craft and earns a voice through professional work.
- How he became who he is: fought his way from an outsourcing firm to a core role — "being dismissed" once cost him dearly.
- Life meaning: prove with his work that he deserves to be taken seriously.

---

## 📊 My Insight & Conclusion

- **The layer I can reliably reach**: barely Layer 5 this time — in earlier conflicts I stopped at Layer 1 ("he has a bad attitude").
- **Where I get stuck and why**: Layer 3 — surface anger is deceptive. Anger is the shield; the hurt is the spear.
- **Next step**: find him 1:1 on Monday. Don't explain last week's requirements — acknowledge first: "The last-minute changes derailed your prep twice. I'm sorry." Then agree together on a lead-time rule for requirement sync.
