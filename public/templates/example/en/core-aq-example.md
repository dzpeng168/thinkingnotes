# Walking Out of a Failed Promotion with the LEAD Tool

> **CORE AQ Model · the LEAD four steps for facing adversity.**
> View your adversity quotient on four dimensions — Control, Ownership, Reach, Endurance — then run the LEAD steps to pull your mindset back on track.

**Current adversity**: I failed the annual promotion review — my second rejection.

---

## L · Listen — listen to your reaction to the adversity

- What is happening? My score was 3.6 against a 4.0 bar; the panel's feedback: "insufficient evidence of cross-team influence."
- What do I feel inside at this moment? What changes in my body? First reaction: shame and anger; a tight chest; doom-scrolling all evening. This morning the thought of facing colleagues made my stomach clench.
- Why do I feel this way? What's behind it? The shame comes from "I worked a full year only to be told I'm not good enough" — I turned a promotion review into a verdict on me as a person, instead of feedback on one rating.

## E · Explore — explore your responsibility in the adversity

- In this matter, what do I truly care about? Not the title or the raise — being seen and acknowledged for my work.
- Which parts of the outcome should I take responsibility for? True: 90% of my output this year stayed inside the team, with near-zero lateral visibility. That was my choice of working style, and the consequence is mine.
- Which parts should NOT be my responsibility? Promotion slots tightened to 20%; my manager never once created cross-team exposure for me. Those are not my failure.

## A · Analyze — analyze objectively, break limiting beliefs

- What evidence shows I cannot control this? None. Visibility is fully within my control — next year I can claim cross-team projects and share work publicly.
- What evidence shows this predicament will definitely spread to other areas of life? None. It affects promotion timing, not my health, relationships, or skills. "It's over for me" is just the emotion talking.
- What evidence shows this predicament will continue indefinitely? None. A colleague who failed twice passed on the third try by bringing a cross-team project. The panel gave an improvement path, not a death sentence.

## D · Do — do something to make the situation better

- What can I do to increase the possibility of things getting better? Claim a joint project with the monetization team next quarter; run a tech talk once a quarter to build "cross-team influence" evidence.
- What can I do to control the scope of the impact? Set a "no rumination" alarm — after 9 p.m., the promotion topic is off-limits.
- What can I do to gain a little sense of control? Book 30 minutes with my manager this week to translate the panel feedback into three concrete moves for next year.

---

## 📊 Retrospective

- Emotion shift after the four steps: shame and helplessness dropped from an 8 to a 3 — the E step, drawing the line between "mine to own" and "not mine to own", dissolved most of the anger.
- Re-assessment of control: this rejection is 30% environment, 70% a changeable working-style choice — a far higher controllable share than I believed.
- Smallest next action: send the calendar invite to my manager this afternoon.
