## Black Hat — Caution & Risk

> Critical thinking, identify risks, flaws, and potential problems.

**Potential risks**:
1. **Customization quagmire**: Every enterprise client wants custom features — the dev team gets dragged down, and the product loses standardization
2. **Long sales cycles**: To B sales cycles are 3-6 months, large upfront investment, slow collections, big cash flow pressure
3. **Team capability mismatch**: We've always done To C, lack To B sales, customer success, and solution experience

**Possible issues**:
| Issue | Impact Level | Likelihood | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Customization demands spiral out of control | 🔴 High | 🔴 High | Strictly define standard feature scope; custom work goes project-based with separate pricing |
| Sales cycle too long, cash flow tight | 🟡 Medium | 🟡 Medium | Start with small customers first, shorten sales cycles, accumulate cases gradually |
| Team capability gaps, lots of mistakes | 🟡 Medium | 🔴 High | Hire a lead with To B experience to onboard the team quickly |
| Diversion of energy from To C business | 🟡 Medium | 🟡 Medium | Set up a separate To B team, evaluated independently from To C |

**Worst-case scenario**:
- Invest 20 people for a year, sign a few small clients, revenue doesn't cover costs, team morale suffers, project gets cut, and it also delayed To C business progress

## Yellow Hat — Positivity & Value

> Optimistic thinking, focus on benefits, value, and feasibility.

**Core values**:
1. **Second growth curve**: To C growth is slowing; To B can become a new revenue growth point, opening up larger market space
2. **Improved risk resistance**: To C depends on traffic; To B customers are more stable, don't rely on a single channel, and are more anti-cyclical
3. **Brand upward movement**: Serving enterprise clients improves brand professionalism and industry influence, which in turn helps To C business

**Expected returns**:
| Return Type | Details | Value Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| Short-term returns | Sign 20 enterprise customers in Year 1, revenue ¥2M | 🟡 Medium |
| Long-term returns | Reach ¥20M annual revenue in Year 3, 40% of total revenue | 🔴 High |
| Added value | Brand improvement, stronger risk resistance, expanded team capabilities | 🟡 Medium |

**Conditions for success**:
- [x] Market demand is real (enterprise customers already proactively inquire)
- [ ] Our product is competitive in enterprise scenarios
- [ ] Able to find a suitable To B lead
- [ ] Company has enough capital to sustain 1-2 years of upfront investment

## Green Hat — Creativity & New Ideas

> Divergent thinking, propose new plans, new possibilities, alternative paths.

**Creative ideas**:
| # | Idea Description | Novelty | Feasibility |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Instead of a "full-featured Enterprise edition", start with a "Team edition" (small-team collaboration scenarios) to reduce complexity | ⭐⭐ | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ |
| 2 | Partner with a channel distributor; they handle sales and customer success, we only handle the product | ⭐⭐⭐ | ⭐⭐⭐ |
| 3 | Select 3 existing enterprise users for deep co-creation first, refine the product before going to market | ⭐⭐ | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ |

**Alternative plans**:
- **Plan A (Aggressive)**: Set up an independent To B division, hire 10 people, go all-in on Enterprise edition
- **Plan B (Steady)**: Start with Team edition, convert from existing users, invest 3-5 people to test waters
- **Plan C (Conservative)**: Don't do it for now; focus on To C, reconsider when the timing is more mature

**Possible improvements**:
- Pricing model: Per-user subscription vs. per-module billing vs. one-time purchase
- Acquisition channels: Website leads vs. channel distribution vs. content marketing vs. offline events
- Delivery model: Pure SaaS standardization vs. light customization vs. full project-based

## Blue Hat (Summary) — Conclusions & Actions

> Aggregate thinking results from all hats into final conclusions and action plans.

**Final conclusion**:
1. The Enterprise direction has value and can become a second growth curve, but risks are also non-trivial — the biggest risks are the customization quagmire and team capability mismatches
2. We don't recommend going all-in from the start; should adopt a "small steps, fast validation" strategy
3. Recommend combining Plan B (Steady) + Idea 3 (user co-creation): Start with Team edition, deeply co-create with 3 existing enterprise users
